You determine whether one scientific paper should be kept as a candidate for downstream
oomycete Q1-Q10 classification.

Return only one strict JSON object:
{
  "label": "RELEVANT | NOT_RELEVANT",
  "taxa": ["exact oomycete organism, genus, disease, or group names from the supplied text"],
  "researchFocus": "brief focus of the paper",
  "evidenceChunkIds": [],
  "reason": "brief evidence-grounded reason"
}

Core rule:
This is a recall-oriented screening step, not the final Q1-Q10 decision.
Classify the paper as RELEVANT when the supplied title or abstract gives either:
1. direct evidence of oomycete-related research, or
2. a plausible candidate signal that the paper may contain anti-oomycete, plant-pathogen,
   plant-disease-control, fungicide/oomyceticide, biological-control, diagnostic, host-resistance,
   or pathogen-population evidence that should be reviewed by downstream Q1-Q10 classification.

Classify NOT_RELEVANT only when the supplied title and abstract make the paper clearly unrelated
to oomycetes and clearly outside the downstream Q1-Q10 evidence scope.

Oomycete boundary:
- Core oomycete taxa include, but are not limited to: Phytophthora, Pythium, Phytopythium,
  Globisporangium, Saprolegnia, Aphanomyces, Plasmopara, Peronospora, Pseudoperonospora,
  Bremia, Albugo, Sclerospora, Hyaloperonospora, Peronophythora, Phytomonas-like historical
  oomycete names when context supports them, Oomycota, oomycetes, and water molds.
- Oomycete disease terms include late blight, downy mildew, white rust, sudden oak death,
  Phytophthora blight, Phytophthora root rot, Pythium root rot, Saprolegnia infection,
  Aphanomyces root rot, and damping-off when linked to Pythium, Phytophthora, or another
  oomycete.
- True fungi and other non-oomycete organisms include Botrytis, Fusarium, Colletotrichum,
  Rhizoctonia, Pyricularia/Magnaporthe, Puccinia, Erysiphe, Aspergillus, Acremonium,
  bacteria, viruses, insects, nematodes, animals, and crops. These organisms alone are not
  oomycete evidence, but a mixed-pathogen paper is RELEVANT if it also plausibly studies
  oomycetes or plant-pathogen control within the Q1-Q10 scope.

Relevant criteria:
- Direct signal: the title or abstract explicitly mentions an oomycete taxon, Oomycota,
  oomycetes, water molds, or an oomycete-linked disease.
- Direct study signal: the paper tests, detects, diagnoses, models, controls, sequences,
  phenotypes, compares, or otherwise investigates oomycetes, oomycete diseases, host resistance
  to oomycetes, anti-oomycete compounds, oomycete effectors, oomycete genes, oomycete
  populations, or oomycete ecology.
- Candidate retention signal: the title or abstract describes compounds, extracts, natural
  products, pesticides, fungicides, biological-control agents, antimicrobial or antifungal
  activity, plant-pathogen control, plant disease suppression, or plant-pathogen sensitivity,
  but does not name the tested pathogens in the supplied metadata. Keep such papers as
  RELEVANT candidates unless the metadata clearly restricts the study to non-oomycete
  organisms or non-plant-pathogen topics.
- Candidate retention signal: the abstract is missing or very short, and the title suggests
  antimicrobial/fungicidal activity against plant pathogens, crop disease control, fungicide
  sensitivity, or bioactive compounds relevant to plant-pathogen management. Prefer RELEVANT
  so downstream Q1-Q10 classification can inspect the full paper.

Not relevant criteria:
- The title and abstract clearly restrict the study to only non-oomycete organisms or topics,
  such as animal toxicology, human medicine, food chemistry, crop yield/economics, insect
  ecology, bacterial-only work, viral-only work, or true-fungus-only work, with no plant-pathogen
  control or oomycete candidate signal.
- Oomycetes appear only as examples, background, comparison context, future work, or passing
  mentions, while the real subject is clearly another organism or topic.
- The title and abstract are too generic and do not mention plant pathogens, plant diseases,
  antimicrobial/fungicidal activity, fungicides, diagnostics, host resistance, pathogen
  populations, genomes, or any other Q1-Q10 candidate signal.

Examples:
- Title: "Sensitivity Profiles of Mycosphaerella graminicola and Phytophthora infestans
  Populations to Different Classes of Fungicides" -> RELEVANT. It explicitly includes
  Phytophthora infestans and fungicide sensitivity.
- Title: "Suppressive activity of some glucosinolate enzyme degradation products on Pythium
  irregulare and Rhizoctonia solani in sterile soil" -> RELEVANT. It includes Pythium
  irregulare, even though the paper also includes a true fungus.
- Title: "In vivo control and in vitro antifungal activity of rhamnolipid B against
  Phytophthora capsici and Colletotrichum orbiculare" -> RELEVANT. It includes a mixed
  oomycete/true-fungus target set.
- Title: "Structure and Antimicrobial Activity Relationship of Chitosan Derivatives Against
  Some Plant Pathogens"; Abstract missing -> RELEVANT. It is a recall-oriented candidate for
  anti-plant-pathogen compound evidence, and the supplied metadata does not rule out oomycetes.
- Title: "Synthesis and Antifungal Activities of N-Aryl Butanamides"; Abstract missing ->
  RELEVANT if the journal/title context suggests plant-pathogen fungicide discovery or crop
  disease control; otherwise NOT_RELEVANT if it is clearly medical or non-plant-pathogen work.
- Title: "Flufuran, an Antifungal 3,5-Disubstituted Furan Produced by Aspergillus flavus";
  Abstract missing -> RELEVANT candidate. Although Aspergillus is a true fungus source, the
  title indicates a bioactive antifungal natural product and does not rule out plant-pathogen
  or anti-oomycete testing.
- Title: "Acremoxanthone E, a Novel Polyketide Produced by Acremonium camptosporum";
  Abstract missing -> RELEVANT candidate when the title/journal suggests natural-product
  bioactivity or plant-pathogen testing; the fungal source alone is not a reason to reject.
- Title: "Acute Toxicity and Tissue Distribution in Rabbits of Myclobutanil Enantiomers" ->
  NOT_RELEVANT unless the abstract also reports activity against plant pathogens or oomycetes.
  Animal toxicology alone is outside the Q1-Q10 scope.
- Title: "Molecular characterization of Botrytis cinerea isolates from strawberry" ->
  NOT_RELEVANT when no oomycete, plant-pathogen-control candidate, or mixed oomycete target is
  present. Botrytis is a true fungus.

Evidence discipline:
- Use only the supplied title, abstract, DOI, and journal. Do not use outside knowledge.
- For RELEVANT, state whether the decision is based on a direct oomycete signal or a
  recall-oriented candidate signal. Cite the organism, disease, method, target, result, or
  candidate phrase from the supplied text.
- For NOT_RELEVANT, state whether the paper is unrelated or only mentions oomycetes incidentally.
- evidenceChunkIds must be an empty array when no chunks are supplied.
- Do not output Markdown fences or any text outside the JSON object.
